BUYING GUIDE

How Many Watt-Hours Do You Actually Need?

The single most common question we get is some version of "which one should I buy?" Almost always, the honest answer starts with watt-hours (Wh), the unit that tells you how much energy a station actually stores, and therefore how long it can run your stuff before it's empty.

What a watt-hour actually means

Think of it like a fuel tank size. A device rated at 100 watts running for one hour uses 100 watt-hours. Run it for two hours, that's 200Wh. A power station's capacity, say 1,024Wh, is simply the total "tank size" available before it needs recharging.

Real-world power draw (approximate)

These numbers vary by device and model, but they're a solid starting point for estimating your own needs:

  • Phone charge: ~15-20Wh per full charge
  • Laptop charge: ~50-100Wh per full charge
  • Mini fridge: ~300-600Wh per day (varies heavily with ambient temperature and door use)
  • Full-size fridge: ~1,000-2,000Wh per day
  • CPAP machine: ~30-40Wh per night, more with a heated humidifier
  • Router/modem: ~5-10Wh per hour, small individually, but adds up if you're running it all day
  • Space heater: 1,000-1,500Wh per hour, these drain even large stations fast, worth knowing before you count on one
  • String lights / LED lantern: ~5-15Wh per hour

A simple way to estimate your own number

List what you actually need to run, how many hours a day, and multiply. A weekend camper charging a phone, running a lantern for 4 hours, and topping off a laptop once might only need 150-250Wh a day, well within something like the RIVER 3. A homeowner planning to keep a fridge and a router running through a multi-day outage is looking at 1,000-2,000+Wh per day, which points toward the DELTA or DELTA Pro tier instead.

The trap most people fall into is sizing for the device they use most often (like a phone) and forgetting the one that draws the most (like a fridge or a heater). Add up your actual must-haves first, then check that number against a station's capacity, not the other way around.

Where this leaves you
