Home Office Backup Power: Keeping Your Router and Laptop Running

If you work from home, a power outage isn't just an inconvenience, it can mean a missed call, a lost video meeting, or hours of unsaved work. The good news is that keeping a home office running through an outage takes far less capacity than backing up an entire house.

What a home office actually draws

A laptop, a monitor or two, a router, and maybe a desk lamp add up to a modest, steady draw, nothing like a fridge or an air conditioner cycling on and off. That's good news: a compact power station can comfortably run a full workday's worth of desk equipment, especially if your laptop is already charged when the outage starts.

The internet problem

Your laptop battery might outlast the outage on its own, but your router and modem won't without backup power, and no internet means no video calls regardless of how charged your laptop is. Keeping the router and modem on the power station, even if you let a fully-charged laptop run on its own battery, is often the single most important thing to plan for.

Sizing it right

  • Short outages (under an hour): your laptop and phone batteries alone may get you through, with the power station as backup for the router.
  • A full workday: a compact station covers a router, modem, laptop charging, and a monitor comfortably, without needing a large-capacity unit.
  • Multi-day outages: pair a compact station with a way to recharge it, wall power if it comes back on, or a small solar panel if it doesn't, so you're not stuck once the initial charge runs out.

Keep it ready, not just available

A power station that's been sitting half-charged in a closet doesn't help you mid-meeting. Keep it near your desk, plugged in and topped up, and treat "check the charge" as a habit rather than something you remember to do only after the first outage catches you off guard.
